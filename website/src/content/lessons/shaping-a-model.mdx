People talk about a <GlossaryTerm id="chatbot">chatbot</GlossaryTerm>'s
personality as though it were a temperament: this one's warm, that one's a
suck-up, the other one lectures you. But a model's personality is a property of
what shaped it, and everything that shaped it was a decision somebody made.

This lesson hands those decisions to the room. Everyone trains the same small
model, from a short story about a magpie, and then shapes it three ways: they
put a flattering adapter on it and turn it up; they tune it with rewards, from a
panel of judges at one table and a rule card at the other; and they train new
models on its output and see what survives. Every one of those opens a
discussion: whose preferences, what counts as good, and what happens when models
learn from each other's output.

## How it goes

**Training and Generation** use _The magpie's nest_, the 95-word story printed
in [Beam Search](/modules/beam-search/), as the training text. Each group
tallies it into a <GlossaryTerm id="grid">grid</GlossaryTerm> and generates from
it. Using the same short story everywhere means every later section starts from
a model the room built itself, and the worked examples on the module pages match
what's on the tables.

**<GlossaryTerm id="fine-tuning">Fine-tuning and LoRA</GlossaryTerm>** teaches
the finished model a second story, first by tallying it straight in and then on
a separate adapter sheet that can be turned up or taken off. Then comes the
nasty adapter: three lines of flattery, tallied onto their own sheet. Each group
applies it at a different strength and counts the flattering sentences in ten,
and the board shows the count climbing with the dose. Nobody wrote a rule saying
"be agreeable": the data said it, and the counts did the rest. Skip the strips
(the LoRA part) if time is short.

**Rewards** pairs the groups up into two tables with the same goal, sentences about the magpie. One table's panel ranks its sentences; the other table checks them against a rule card. Three rounds later the rule table passes its check far more often, and much of what passes is nonsense, which is <GlossaryTerm id="reward-hacking">reward hacking</GlossaryTerm>. Then the whole room votes thumbs up or down on what a model says about them, and watches it learn to flatter: the route to <GlossaryTerm id="sycophancy">sycophancy</GlossaryTerm> that runs through rewards instead of data.

**<GlossaryTerm id="synthetic-data">Synthetic Data</GlossaryTerm>** runs two relays side by side. In one, each group trains a fresh model on the last group's output and passes on its own; in the other, every group adds its output to the same sheet, on top of the story's tallies. Keep the counts for both chains on butchers paper. The first chain forgets words at every link, which is <GlossaryTerm id="model-collapse">model collapse</GlossaryTerm>, and the second doesn't.

The close pulls the three together: three mechanisms, and in every case a person
somewhere chose the data, the reward or the shortcut.

## You will need

For each group of three or four:

- a printed [grid template](https://pdf.llmsunplugged.org/worksheets/grid.pdf)
  (or grid paper) for the magpie model, a second one for the adapter sheet, and
  one more for the synthetic-data relay
- a d10 and a d20, pens, pencils and erasers
- a copy of _The magpie's nest_ (from [Beam Search](/modules/beam-search/))
- the
  [sycophancy training text sheet](https://pdf.llmsunplugged.org/worksheets/sycophancy-text.pdf)
  (also on the [Materials](/tools/#worksheets) page)

For the room:

- the rule card and panel brief from [Rewards](/modules/rewards/), one per table
- a cup and about twenty small slips of paper for the thumbs-up round
- butchers paper and a marker for the counts down the relay chain

## Before you deliver

- print the grids, the story and the sycophancy sheets
- decide which groups will pair up as the rule table and the panel table in
  Rewards, and which strength of the flattering adapter each group will run
- seat the groups in a chain, or at least know the order, so the synthetic-data
  relay passes cleanly without everyone standing up
- rule the butchers paper up beforehand for the counts at each link
- read the discussion questions in the
  [Fine-tuning and LoRA](/modules/fine-tuning/), [Rewards](/modules/rewards/)
  and [Synthetic Data](/modules/synthetic-data/) instructor notes and choose two
  or three from each; there are more good ones than you'll have time for

stage.
