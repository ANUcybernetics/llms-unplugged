People talk about a <GlossaryTerm id="chatbot">chatbot</GlossaryTerm>'s
personality as though it were a temperament: this one's warm, that one's a
suck-up, the other one lectures you. But a model's personality is a property of
what shaped it, and everything that shaped it was a decision somebody made.

This lesson hands those decisions to the room. Everyone trains the same small
model, from a short story about a magpie, and then shapes it three ways: they
put a flattering adapter on it and turn it up; they tune it with rewards, from a
panel of judges at one table and a rule card at the other; and they train new
models on its output and see what survives. Every one of those opens a
discussion: whose preferences, what counts as good, and what happens when models
learn from each other's output.

## How it goes

**Training and Generation** use _The magpie's nest_, the 95-word story printed
in [Beam Search](/modules/beam-search/), as the training text. Each group
tallies it into a <GlossaryTerm id="grid">grid</GlossaryTerm> and generates from
it. Every later section uses the same story, so the worked examples on the
module pages match what's on the tables. Fine-tuning leaves each group's grid as
it is. Rewards and Synthetic Data both change the counts, so for those two each
group works on a printed copy of the same model.

**<GlossaryTerm id="fine-tuning">Fine-tuning and LoRA</GlossaryTerm>** teaches
the finished model a second story. Show the first way, tallying it straight in,
at the front on the `the` row only: it can't be undone, and the groups need
their grids as they are. Then each group puts the story on a separate adapter
sheet that can be turned up or taken off. Then comes the nasty adapter: three
lines of flattery, tallied onto their own sheet. Each group counts the adapter
once, three times or nine times and counts the flattering sentences in ten, and
the board shows the count climbing with the dose. Nobody wrote a rule saying
"be agreeable": the data said it, and the counts did the rest. Skip the strips
(the LoRA part) if time is short.

**Rewards** gives every group the same goal, sentences about the magpie, and one of two ways to judge them. Half the groups rank their sentences as a panel; the other half check them against a rule card. Three rounds later the rule-card groups pass their check far more often, and much of what passes is nonsense, which is <GlossaryTerm id="reward-hacking">reward hacking</GlossaryTerm>. Then the whole room votes thumbs up or down on what a model says about them, and watches it learn to flatter: the route to <GlossaryTerm id="sycophancy">sycophancy</GlossaryTerm> that runs through rewards instead of data.

**<GlossaryTerm id="synthetic-data">Synthetic Data</GlossaryTerm>** runs two kinds of relay at once. Every group starts a chain and passes it one group along at each link, so nobody waits. In a replace chain, each group trains a fresh model on the last group's output and passes on its own; in an accumulate chain, every group adds its output to the same sheet, on top of the story's tallies. Keep the counts for every chain on butchers paper. The replace chains forget words at every link, which is <GlossaryTerm id="model-collapse">model collapse</GlossaryTerm>, and the accumulate chains don't.

The close pulls the three together: three mechanisms, and in every case a person
somewhere chose the data, the reward or the shortcut.

## You will need

For each group of three or four:

- three printed
  [48-word grids](https://pdf.llmsunplugged.org/worksheets/grid-48.pdf): one for
  the magpie model, which has 32 words and needs room for the new ones, and one
  for each of the two adapter sheets
- two [32-word grids](https://pdf.llmsunplugged.org/worksheets/grid.pdf) (or
  grid paper) for the synthetic-data relay
- a d6 and a d20, pens, pencils and erasers
- a copy of _The magpie's nest_ (from [Beam Search](/modules/beam-search/))
- two printed copies of the story's model, the table in
  [Rewards](/modules/rewards/#the-magpies-nest): one to change in Rewards and
  one for Synthetic Data
- the
  [sycophancy training text sheet](https://pdf.llmsunplugged.org/worksheets/sycophancy-text.pdf)
  (also on the [Materials](/tools/#worksheets) page)

For the room:

- a rule card or a panel brief from [Rewards](/modules/rewards/) for each group
- for the thumbs-up round, a cup and about twenty-five small slips of paper for
  every six people
- butchers paper and a marker for the counts down the relay chains

## Before you deliver

- print the grids, the story, the model tables and the sycophancy sheets
- decide which groups get the rule card and which the panel brief in Rewards,
  and which strength of the flattering adapter each group will run
- know the order the groups pass round in, so the synthetic-data relay passes
  cleanly without everyone standing up
- rule the butchers paper up beforehand, with a row for each chain and a column
  for each link
- read the discussion questions in the
  [Fine-tuning and LoRA](/modules/fine-tuning/), [Rewards](/modules/rewards/)
  and [Synthetic Data](/modules/synthetic-data/) instructor notes and choose two
  or three from each; there are more good ones than you'll have time for
