A sibling of [How AI writes stories](/lessons/how-ai-writes-stories/) that swaps
the paper <GlossaryTerm id="cutouts">cutouts</GlossaryTerm> for **<GlossaryTerm id="ledger">ledger sheets</GlossaryTerm>**: a table with one row per word, tally
marks beside each word that followed it, and a coloured strip beside every
tally. <GlossaryTerm id="generation">Generation</GlossaryTerm> is a bag of coloured counters---one counter per tally mark, in
the strip's colour, draw one and read the word beside that colour---so the maths
of "more common means more likely" is done by the bag. <GlossaryTerm id="training">Training</GlossaryTerm> is tallying by
hand from a printed text.

The order is flipped on purpose. Groups start with a **finished** model and
generate from it, so when they train their own from a new text they already know
what every mark is for. Every group's finished model is a different picture
book, so the shareback can hear the book in each group's story.

## Lesson plan

Allow five minutes for arrival and settling before part 1. Part 6 is designed to
be dropped if you're running over.

| Part | What happens                                                           | Time   |
| ---- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------ |
| 1    | **What is AI?** --- discussion, plus an AI writes the class a story    | 12 min |
| 2    | **Warm-up** --- predict the next word, then four key words             | 8 min  |
| 3    | **Read the sheet** --- one row read aloud as a sentence                | 5 min  |
| 4    | **Generation** --- stories from the pre-tallied sheets, then shareback | 20 min |
| 5    | **Training** --- tally a new text onto blank sheets, generate again    | 22 min |
| 6    | **One story, all together** --- the class pools its models (optional)  | 10 min |
| 7    | **Wrap-up** --- what did we learn? Q&A                                 | 5 min  |

**Part 3** is the beat the cutouts lesson doesn't have: before anyone touches a
counter, a whole sheet goes up on the screen, then one row of it is read aloud as
a sentence ("after _I_, this book said _am_ eight times"). Every group then finds
a row of their own and does the same. The colours are explained last: a strip's
colour names its column, and the counters come in those colours.

**Part 4** runs the [Generation](/modules/generation/) module with the bag. The
slides walk through one book (_Green Eggs and Ham_) draw by draw, then every
group generates from its own. The shareback asks the room to guess each group's
book from its first sentence, and asks two groups holding the same book why
their stories differ.

**Part 5** answers "where did the marks come from?" by having each group make
their own. Every group gets a different short text (the five school-day texts
that ship with the CLI) and a set of sheets with the words printed and the
strips empty; one person reads the text two words at a time from the numbered
printout and the group tallies. A short generation round follows, from the word
_the_.

**Part 6** pools the models, on butchers paper up the front. Call out the word
the class story is on; every group whose sheets have a row for it puts a hand up;
pick one, and that group draws from its own bag to give the next word. The class
story can contain words only one group's text had, so it says things no single
group's model could.

## You will need

- one pre-tallied picture-book set per group, five sheets and eight colours, a
  different book per group: `green-eggs-and-ham` (the book the slides walk
  through), `the-very-hungry-caterpillar`, `were-going-on-a-bear-hunt`,
  `fun-with-dick-and-jane` and `the-cat-in-the-hat`
- one training set per group, each from a different
  `data/originals/school-day-*.txt` text, with the words printed and the strips
  empty, plus its `text.pdf`
- counters in the palette's eight colours (red, blue, green, yellow, pink,
  purple, black, white), at least eight of each per group (the largest tally in
  any set), a cloth bag per group, and a tub per group to tip the bag back into
  between draws
- butchers paper for the class story
- pen and paper per group, a whiteboard, a projector, and access to a <GlossaryTerm id="chatbot">chatbot</GlossaryTerm>
  for part 1

## Printing it

`make pack-how-ai-writes-stories-ledger` in the
[repo](https://github.com/ANUcybernetics/llms-unplugged) builds every set above
and zips them into `out/packs/`, with a README naming what to print and how many
copies. The `Makefile` holds the exact commands, so the sets are the same every
delivery rather than rebuilt from memory.

Every picture-book set is built with `--max-followers 4`, which drops each
word's rarest continuations so that every word fits a single row however long
the text. The token budget is then free to be chosen for the model rather than
for the widest row: 140 for four of the books, and 100 for _Green Eggs and Ham_,
which the slides walk through row by row and so has to stay the set they were
written against. Change either number and re-run `ops/ledger-sweep.py` --- its
`dead` column is the one that matters, since a group that draws a word with no
row of its own stalls with nothing to look up.

## Before you deliver

- with more than five groups, books and school-day texts repeat; spread the
  repeats so that groups sharing a book aren't side by side
- the story slide in part 1 has fill-in boxes for the teacher's name, the year
  level, and the three class words
