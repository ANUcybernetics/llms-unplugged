A sibling of [How AI writes stories](/lessons/how-ai-writes-stories/) that
swaps the paper cutouts for **ledger sheets**: a table with one row per word,
tally marks beside each word that followed it, and a coloured strip beside
every tally. Generation is a bag of soft coloured balls---one ball per tally
mark, in the strip's colour, draw one and read the word beside that
colour---so the maths of "more common means more likely" is done by the bag.
Training is tallying by hand from a printed text.

The order is flipped on purpose. Groups start with a **finished** model and
generate from it, so when they train their own from a new text they already
know what every mark is for.

## Lesson plan

Allow five minutes for arrival and settling before part 1. Part 6 is designed
to be dropped if you're running over.

| Part | What happens                                                           | Time   |
| ---- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------ |
| 1    | **What is AI?** --- discussion, plus an AI writes the class a story    | 12 min |
| 2    | **Warm-up** --- predict the next word, then four key words             | 8 min  |
| 3    | **Read the sheet** --- one row read aloud as a sentence                | 5 min  |
| 4    | **Generation** --- stories from the pre-tallied sheets, then shareback | 20 min |
| 5    | **Training** --- tally a new text onto blank sheets, generate again    | 25 min |
| 6    | **One story, all together** --- the class pools its models (optional)  | 10 min |
| 7    | **Wrap-up** --- what did we learn? Q&A                                 | 5 min  |

**Part 3** is the beat the cutouts lesson doesn't have: before anyone touches
a ball, one row of the sheet is read aloud as a sentence ("after _I_, this
text said _do_ nine times"). Every group then finds a row of their own and
does the same. The colours are explained last: a strip's colour names its
column, and the balls come in those colours.

**Part 4** runs the [Generation](/modules/generation/) module with the bag.
Every group starts from the same word, so the shareback can ask why five
groups holding the same model wrote five different stories.

**Part 5** answers "where did the marks come from?" by having each group make
their own. Every group gets a different short text (the five school-day texts
that ship with the CLI) and a set of sheets with the words printed and the
strips empty; one person reads the text two words at a time from the numbered
printout and the group tallies. A short generation round follows, from the
word _the_.

**Part 6** pools the models. A bucket goes in the middle of the room, each
group has a ball colour, and for the current word every group with a row for
it throws in one ball per mark on that row. The ball drawn picks the group;
that group draws from its own bag to pick the word. Groups whose text used a
word more often get more say, and the class story can contain words only one
group's text had.

## You will need

- the pre-tallied _Green Eggs and Ham_ set, one per group: the first 150
  tokens, five sheets, two palettes (`llms_unplugged ledger -i
data/green-eggs-and-ham.txt --max-tokens 150 --sheets 5 --palettes 2
--prefill tallies`)
- one training set per group, each from a different `data/school-day-*.txt`
  text, printed with `--prefill prefixes --palettes 2`, plus its `text.pdf`
- soft balls in the eight colours of the first two palettes (red, blue, green,
  yellow, pink, purple, black, white), at least eleven of each per group, and
  a cloth bag per group
- a bucket for the room, and butchers paper for the class story
- pen and paper per group, a whiteboard, a projector, and access to a chatbot
  for part 1

## Before you deliver

- run the sweep script (`ops/ledger-sweep.py`) if you change the text or its
  length: no prefix may run past two rows with eight colours, and the largest
  tally count is how many balls of one colour a group can need
- give every group a different school-day text---the finale has nothing to
  pool if they all trained on the same one
- the story slide in part 1 has fill-in boxes for the teacher's name, the year
  level, and the three class words
