A talk, not a workshop: the only thing the audience does is hold a
[search sheet](/modules/search-sheets/) and put a hand up. The room runs a
bigram model of _The Cat in the Hat_ for five minutes, maps what it did onto the
real vocabulary (tokens, parameters, distribution, sampling), sees the same
model in grid form, and then watches three scales grow---context, training text,
learned numbers---from the paper model to a frontier LLM, without pretending the
two are the same kind of machine. It closes on the claim: you can't build
Claude, but twenty minutes of paper and hands in the air makes you harder to
fool.

We gave it at ANU in August 2026, straight after Tim Bell's introduction to CS
Unplugged, which is where its opening Bandura quote comes from.

## You will need

- one search sheet per attendee, sized to the head count: the ready-to-print
  [Cat in the Hat set](/tools/#search-sheets) holds 36 sheets (or A5 two-up), or
  deal your own with
  `llms_unplugged sheets -i data/the-cat-in-the-hat.txt -n 2 --sheets <count>`
- the brief (page 1 of the PDF) on the lectern; it carries the worked example
- the deck's whiteboard overlay (press `b`) for the generated text, and a scribe
  if you'd rather not write and talk
- a projector for the slides

## Before you deliver

- don't hand the sheets out early; they get read, and the "nobody here is
  holding a story" beat depends on the shuffle
- the numbers on the "between us we have the whole book" and scale slides are
  for _The Cat in the Hat_ at n=2; if you swap the corpus, rebuild them with
  `llms_unplugged build` and read the metadata block
- the Bandura framing and the "come to tomorrow's workshop" close were for that
  event; the middle fourteen minutes stand on their own
