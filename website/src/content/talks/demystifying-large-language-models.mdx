TED-style: the speaking carries it and the slides are cues, not visual aids. The
audience is handed [search sheets](/modules/search-sheets/) as the house lights
come up, runs the model for four minutes by show of hands, and then learns what
it just did. The spine is a reveal: the three source books are never named until
near the end, and nothing on the printed sheets names them either, so a hall
that has just generated "the old man sat in the hat" finds out it was Hemingway,
Poe and Dr Seuss---in that order, because Seuss is the biggest recognition hit
and has to land last.

We gave it at the ANU Visionaries Among Us Showcase in August 2026, to a hall of
about 360. The deck below is that talk with the event-specific slides taken
out; the deck as delivered is linked alongside it.

## Running order

Target thirteen minutes against a fifteen-minute slot; the run is where the
spare time goes.

| Time  | Beat                                                | Slides |
| ----- | --------------------------------------------------- | ------ |
| 0:00  | the tool before the story; two stories              | 1--3   |
| 2:00  | look inside (sheets go out)                         | 4      |
| 3:00  | what you've just been given, then the recipe + seed | 5--6   |
| 5:00  | run the room (four minutes, 12--15 tokens)          | 7      |
| 9:00  | what just happened: the role mapping                | 8--9   |
| 10:00 | the sentence is in no book; three books             | 10--11 |
| 13:00 | close and QR                                        | 12--15 |

Only three slides carry real text: the algorithm, the role mapping and the
three-book reveal. If a slide is tempting you to read it out, cut it rather than
the sentence you were going to say.

## You will need

- one search sheet per seat, dealt from the three texts together, with the title
  and author left off every sheet (the CLI's `sheets` subcommand takes `--title`
  and `--author` overrides); the deck's comment block has the exact recipe, and
  the deal is seeded so it's reproducible
- ushers to hand the sheets out on cue, fast: "take one, pass the rest along"
- a whiteboard, or the deck's annotation layer, to write the growing sentence
  where the back of the hall can read it
- a projector for the slides

## Before you deliver

- rehearse the hand-out: the sheets go out on slide 4, not before, and the run
  can't start until nearly everyone is holding one
- pick loud seeds in case you need to restart; the deck notes list them for each
  text
- write big. Fifteen tokens at whiteboard size is the whole output; the back row
  needs to read it for the reveal to land
- the as-delivered deck has ANU-at-80 slides near the end that were for that
  event; the deck above already leaves them out
