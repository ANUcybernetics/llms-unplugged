Every project needs a logo, and for a project about demystifying how language
models work it felt right to make the logo _itself_ an explainer. So the LLMs
Unplugged logo is built from the thing that language models actually operate
on---tokens.

## Tokenising the title

If you feed the string "LLMs Unplugged" into
[OpenAI's tokeniser](https://platform.openai.com/tokenizer) (the
[cl100k_base](https://github.com/openai/tiktoken) vocabulary used by GPT-3.5/4),
you get five tokens:

| Token  | ID    |
| ------ | ----- |
| `LL`   | 3069  |
| `Ms`   | 5765  |
| ` Un`  | 1252  |
| `plug` | 37729 |
| `ged`  | 2004  |

These splits are a nice illustration of tokenisation in practice. "LLMs" doesn't
stay as one piece---it becomes `LL` + `Ms`, which makes a certain kind of sense
if you squint (capital letters are rare enough that the tokeniser treats them
separately). "Unplugged" becomes ` Un` + `plug` + `ged`, with that leading space
on ` Un` showing how [BPE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byte_pair_encoding)
tokenisers encode word boundaries. None of this is obvious until you actually
look at it, which is kind of the point of the whole project.

## The logo

Each of those five tokens becomes a coloured brick in the logo, arranged on two
lines to spell out the title. They sit against a field of dimmed background
bricks---other tokens from the vocabulary, filling up the space the way tokens
fill up a model's context window.

## The favicon

The favicon takes this one step further. Each token ID is just a number, and
numbers can be written in binary. Token 3069 ("LL") in 16-bit binary is
`0000 1011 1111 1101`. Lay those 16 bits out in a 4×4 grid, colour the 1s in
gold and leave the 0s dim, and you get this:

<img src="/favicon.svg" alt="The LLMs Unplugged favicon---the binary dot pattern for token 3069" style="max-width: 120px; margin: 1.5rem auto; display: block;" />

It's the kind of thing that nobody will ever notice unprompted (pun intended),
but once you know what it is, you can't unsee it. Every dot pattern on the site
is a real token ID rendered the same way.

## The animated version

There's also an animated version of the logo which starts with around 500
token-sized bricks packed into a dense grid---each displaying the binary dot
pattern for its token ID. Then the five title tokens light up in gold. Then they
smoothly slide into position to spell out "LLMs Unplugged", while the background
bricks fade. After a pause, the whole thing reverses and the title dissolves
back into the field of tokens. Each cycle the bricks are re-randomised, so the
title tokens emerge from different positions every time.

The animation captures the core visual metaphor of the project---meaningful text
emerging from what looks like a random jumble of tokens. It's basically what a
language model does, just at a pace humans can follow.
