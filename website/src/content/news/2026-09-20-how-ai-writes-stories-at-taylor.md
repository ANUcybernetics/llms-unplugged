Last Tuesday (15 September), we visited Taylor Primary School to run the new
[ledger version of How AI Writes Stories](/lessons/how-ai-writes-stories-ledger/)
with 70 Year 5/6 kids. The session went well, and we came away with a collection
of hand-built language models.

With the preprinted cutouts, students start with word pairs already prepared for
them. On a ledger, they work through a text and tally which words follow each
other. They can trace each pattern in the text to the marks they've added to
their model, which makes it easier to see what training involves.

To generate text, students put coloured counters in a cup, one per tally mark in
the current row. They draw a counter and read the word beside that colour. More
common continuations get more counters, making them more likely to be drawn.
Students don't need to convert counts into probabilities or map those
probabilities onto dice rolls, as they do with the grids.

Thanks to the Taylor Primary team for having us. If you'd like to try the
ledgers with your students, the
[lesson page has the plan and slides](/lessons/how-ai-writes-stories-ledger/),
or [get in touch](/about/#get-in-touch) to organise a workshop.
