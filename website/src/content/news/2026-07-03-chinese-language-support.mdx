Recently Zhang Xilian, a master's student at The Education University of Hong
Kong, wrote to say she was planning to run LLMs Unplugged workshops in China,
and that the [Training](/lessons/training) and
[Generation](/lessons/generation) widgets didn't understand Mandarin Chinese.
Neither did the booklet generator. She was right: paste a line of Chinese into
any of them and nothing happened.

The reason was buried in the tokeniser. To turn text into a model you first cut
it into tokens, and our tokeniser only recognised the letters `a` to `z`.
Everything else was treated as a gap between words and thrown away: digits,
stray symbols, and, as it turns out, every Chinese character. Feed it a whole
poem and you got an empty model, with nothing to count and nothing to generate
from.

The fix is to tokenise Chinese one character at a time. English hides its word
boundaries in the spaces, but Chinese runs the characters together, so there is
no space to split on. Character-level tokenisation sidesteps that problem, and
it happens to suit the unplugged approach. Each character becomes one token: a
cell in the frequency grid, a cutout card, or a face you can roll for. 莲 follows
采, 叶 follows 莲, and the dice don't care that the tokens are Chinese.

Here's a bigram model trained on _Jiangnan_, a two-thousand-year-old Han-dynasty
folk poem about picking lotus. Press play and watch it count the characters:

Chinese punctuation gets the same treatment. The full-width comma ， and full
stop 。 are kept as their own tokens, boxed in the model just like the English
`.` and `,`. A generated line breaks where a real sentence would.

The same change flows through to print. Here's the
[_Jiangnan_ bigram booklet](/assets/pdfs/booklets/jiangnan.pdf) as a
ready-to-print PDF, with every dice-lookup entry set in Noto Serif CJK rather
than the Latin body font. The [tools page](/tools) will generate one from any
text you paste in, Mandarin or otherwise. The bundled Chinese fonts cover
simplified characters, the everyday script across mainland China.

<Callout type="note" title="What works, and what's next">

The widgets, the booklet generator, the cutouts, and the in-browser PDF export
all handle Chinese now. If you are teaching in another language and something
doesn't work, tell us. The tokeniser is where most of these limits live, and
they are usually a small change once we know they are there.

</Callout>

Thanks again to Xilian for the nudge, and for the reading list of Chinese
classroom texts to test it on. If you run a workshop with any of this, I'd love
to hear how it goes.
