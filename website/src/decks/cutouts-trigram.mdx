# Trigrams

![bg brightness:0.5](./assets/bg-trigrams-banner.avif)

---

## Your goal

![bg right:40%](./assets/bg-insight-trigram.avif)

extend the bigram model: each cutout now has **two** previous words instead of
one

<Cutout prev={["the", "cat"]} next="sat" />

bigram asks _"what follows this word?"_---trigram asks _"what follows these two
words?"_

predictions get "better", but the model size (number of cutouts) goes up too

---

## You will need

![bg right:40%](./assets/bg-trigram-supplies.avif)

**everyone together** (one big spread)

your trained **trigram** cutouts (not just the bigram ones from before)

pen and paper to write down the generated text

---

## Worked example: match both previous words

your spread has cutouts like:

<CutoutRow>
  <Cutout prev={["the", "cat"]} next="sat" />
  <Cutout prev={["the", "cat"]} next="ran" />
  <Cutout prev={["the", "dog"]} next="sat" />
  <Cutout prev={["the", "dog"]} next="ran" />
</CutoutRow>

match the two most recent words you wrote down to the two previous-word boxes
on a cutout---**both** have to match

you wrote <Token next>the</Token> <Token next>cat</Token>---two cutouts match
(<Cutout prev={["the", "cat"]} next="sat" />, <Cutout prev={["the", "cat"]} next="ran" />)---pick one

---

## Worked example: most matches are unique

![bg right:40%](./assets/bg-trigram-unique-match.avif)

let's say you picked <Token next>sat</Token>---your last two words are now
<Token next>cat</Token> <Token next>sat</Token>---only one cutout matches:

<CutoutRow>
  <Cutout prev={["cat", "sat"]} next="." />
</CutoutRow>

write <Token next>.</Token>---last two words: <Token next>sat</Token>
<Token next>.</Token>---scan for matches---and so on

most two-word combos only have one or two matches

---

## Activity (20 min)

![bg right:40%](./assets/bg-activity-trigram.avif)

swap your bigram cutouts for the trigram cutouts

generate a sentence---does it feel more natural than the bigram output?
