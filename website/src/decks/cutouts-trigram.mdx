# Trigrams

![bg brightness:0.5](./assets/bg-trigrams-banner.avif)

---

## Your goal

![bg right:40%](./assets/bg-insight-trigram.avif)

extend the bigram model: each cutout now has **two** previous words instead of
one

<Cutout prev={["the", "cat"]} next="sat" />

bigram asks _"what follows this word?"_---trigram asks _"what follows these two
words?"_

predictions get sharper, but the spread gets bigger and sparser

---

## You will need

- your trained trigram cutouts (a new set, same text as before)
- pen and paper to write down the generated text

---

## Worked example: match the rightmost box

your spread has cutouts like:

<CutoutRow>
  <Cutout prev={["the", "cat"]} next="sat" />
  <Cutout prev={["the", "cat"]} next="ran" />
  <Cutout prev={["the", "dog"]} next="sat" />
  <Cutout prev={["the", "dog"]} next="ran" />
</CutoutRow>

match the **rightmost** previous-word box to your current word, then check the
box to its left matches the word before that

you wrote <Token next>the</Token> <Token next>cat</Token>---two cutouts match
(<Cutout prev={["the", "cat"]} next="sat" />, <Cutout prev={["the", "cat"]} next="ran" />)---pick one

---

## Worked example: most matches are unique

let's say you picked <Token next>sat</Token>---your last two words are now `cat
sat`---only one cutout matches:

<CutoutRow>
  <Cutout prev={["cat", "sat"]} next="." />
</CutoutRow>

write <Token next>.</Token>---last two words: `sat .`---scan for matches---and
so on

most two-word combos only have one or two matches---trigrams are **sharper but
sparser** than bigrams

---

## Activity (20 min)

![bg right:40%](./assets/bg-activity-trigram.avif)

swap your bigram cutouts for the trigram cutouts of the same text

generate a sentence---does it feel more natural than the bigram output?
