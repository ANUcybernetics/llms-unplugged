## the algorithm

![bg right:40%](./assets/bg-algorithm-generation.avif)

1. I **call out** the last token we wrote (and its colour)
2. **hands up** if it's the _boxed_ token on your page
3. I **pick a hand at random** --- you read out the token beside it
4. the scribe **writes it down**, and we go again

{/* notes:
Get the seed first: ask someone near the front to read out any pair from their page, both tokens, boxed one first. Scribe writes both. It matters that the opening came off a sheet rather than out of my head --- the room supplies the beginning as well as everything after it.

Then walk the four steps once against those two words.

Step 2: one hand each. More than one match, go with the first you spot.

Step 3 is the one to labour, briefly: at random, not whoever shouts first --- that samples the fastest reader, not the text. Random is what makes the room behave like the model.

Don't explain why yet. They'll see it.
*/}

# let's run it

![bg](./assets/bg-div-lessons.avif)

<Timer minutes={4} overlay />

{/* notes:
Four minutes, 12-15 tokens. Keep rounds moving --- no commentary between a hand going up and a token landing on the board.

Narrate exactly two things, once each, without stopping the flow:

The hands are the distribution. Call `the` and a forest goes up; call a rare token and you get two. Nobody here has been taught weighted sampling and the room is doing it anyway.

The pick is a coin toss. Five hands up, and the four I don't take were all real continuations. Different pick, different sentence --- same reason the regenerate button gives you a different answer.

No dead ends exist in this corpus, so there is no running-dry beat. A silent round is an empty seat: say that out loud, it's the previous slide making itself, then reseed from any pair.

Stop while they still want more. Read the sentence back, straight --- the worse it is, the straighter.
*/}

## what role did you play?

![bg right:40%](./assets/bg-core-insight-portrait.avif)

the pairs on your page → the **parameters** of the model

the hands in the air → the **probability distribution**

my random pick → **sampling**

the sentence on the board → **generation**

## it crossed between them without knowing

![bg right:40%](./assets/bg-confluence.avif)

each book was counted **separately** --- no pair spans the join

but they share **`the`**, **`said`**, **`house`**, **`I`**

{/* notes:
Where two books share a context, generation steps from one to the other and nothing marks the crossing. One token of memory is all it has --- it cannot see which book it's in, because "which book" was never something it recorded.

Every strange sentence you have ever got out of a chatbot is this, at scale.
*/}

{/* _class: impact */}

**more** memory · **more** text · **different** machinery

but still **words in** → **words out**

{/* notes:
Three differences, one sentence each, resist explaining any of them --- each has a full lesson on the website.

More memory: ours looked one token back, which is why the sentence wandered. Frontier models look over the whole conversation.

More text: ours read three books. Fifteen trillion tokens is the published figure for a recent open model.

Different machinery: it spreads what it learns across a trillion numbers instead of separate cells, so what it learns about `cat` reaches `dog` --- there's no empty cell to be impossible.

Two footnotes, only if the clock allows. Those trillion numbers are a separate axis from the fifteen trillion tokens: a model can be given more text without being made bigger, and the two get conflated constantly. And ours only ever continues text --- ask it a question and it continues the question. Being something that answers you is another round of training, on conversations, after all the reading is done.

Don't let any of this turn into "a transformer is our grid, enlarged". It isn't: the grid has a cell per pair and the transformer has none, which is exactly why it can say things nobody ever wrote.

None of that changed the loop. Every reply you have ever had from one of these things was produced one token at a time, by what your arm was doing four minutes ago.
*/}
