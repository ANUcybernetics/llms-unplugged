## the algorithm

![bg right:40%](./assets/bg-algorithm-generation-2.avif)

1. find the **row** for the last word written
2. each tally in that row is a **candidate** for the next word
3. load the bag: **one ball per tally**
4. **draw** --- and write the word down

{/* notes:
Now we make it write. Walk the four steps once against the starting word before doing them for real.

The bag is the honest part. I could point at the biggest count and pick it --- but the model doesn't do that, and the reason it doesn't is the reason every reply from ChatGPT is different. It weighs the options and rolls the dice. Two tallies, two balls; one tally, one ball. The bag IS the probability distribution --- we're just going to hold it.
*/}

Assign a ball colour to each candidate out loud ("`old` is blue, `boy` is red, `house` is yellow"), then load by the tallies --- two blue, two red, one yellow --- so the room watches the distribution being built. Shake, theatrical draw, announce the word. The colour-to-word mapping is fresh each round; the scribe writes the drawn word on a blank card and holds it up.

Deterministic rows are the free tempo: "one tally. No bag. The model has no choice here." Take those at speed.

Ten to twelve words, then stop --- while they still want more. The half-built sentence on cards is the cliffhanger for the reading.

If the dream line lands (the old man said, how i wish we had something to do!) --- stop everything and read it twice.
*/}

<p class="corpus-a">“the old man had taught the boy to fish and the boy loved him. no, the old man said.”<br /><span class="corpus-attribution">— Ernest Hemingway, <em>The Old Man and the Sea</em> (1952)</span></p>

<p class="corpus-b">“and i said, how i wish we had something to do! so we sat in the house.”<br /><span class="corpus-attribution">— Dr. Seuss, <em>The Cat in the Hat</em> (1957)</span></p>

{/* notes:
The reveal. Gold was Hemingway. Blue was Dr. Seuss.

Let the recognition land, then make it mean something: the model was never told they were two different books. It sees one word back --- that's all --- so wherever the two vocabularies touch, it steps across the seam without noticing. That's why our sentence lurched between a fishing boat and a rainy afternoon. Every strange sentence you've seen an AI produce is this, at scale: not malfunction --- a model walking across a seam it cannot see.

(If anyone asks later: give it more context --- a thousand words back instead of one --- and it stops blundering across the seam. That's most of what the big models buy with their size.)
*/}
