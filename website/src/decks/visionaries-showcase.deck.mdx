## the algorithm

![bg right:40%](./assets/bg-algorithm-generation.avif)

1. I **call out** the last token we wrote (and its colour)
2. **hands up** if it's the _boxed_ token on your page
3. I **pick a hand at random** --- you read out the token beside it
4. I **write it down**, and we go again

{/* notes:
Get the seed here but write it on the NEXT slide: ink is committed per slide, so anything written on this one is left behind the moment you advance. Ask someone near the front to read out any pair from their page, both tokens, boxed one first. Hold those two words, advance, then shift-W and write them.

It matters that the opening came off a sheet rather than out of my head --- the room supplies the beginning as well as everything after it.

Then walk the four steps once against those two words.

Step 2: one hand each. More than one match, go with the first you spot.

Step 3 is the one to labour, briefly: at random, not whoever shouts first --- that samples the fastest reader, not the text. Random is what makes the room behave like the model.

Don't explain why yet. They'll see it.
*/}

# let's run it

![bg](./assets/bg-div-lessons.avif)

<Timer minutes={4} overlay />

{/* notes:
Shift-W, write the volunteer's two words, and go. Four minutes, 12-15 tokens. Keep rounds moving --- no commentary between a hand going up and a token landing on the screen.

Write big. It has to read from the back of Manning Clark Hall, and the sentence is what slides 7 and 9 point back at.

Narrate exactly two things, once each, without stopping the flow:

The hands are the distribution. Call `the` and a forest goes up; call a rare token and you get two. Nobody here has been taught weighted sampling and the room is doing it anyway.

The pick is a coin toss. Five hands up, and the four I don't take were all real continuations. Different pick, different sentence --- same reason the regenerate button gives you a different answer.

At this size the search is not the bottleneck, hearing the answer is: a common context puts ~100 hands up. Pick near an aisle or the front where a called-out token carries, and repeat the token back before writing it --- a misheard token is the one thing that can quietly derail the sentence.

No dead ends exist in this corpus, so there is no running-dry beat. A silent round is an empty seat: say that out loud --- it makes the point about where the model lives better than saying it did --- then reseed from any pair.

Stop while they still want more. Read the sentence back, straight --- the worse it is, the straighter. This is the slide with room to run long: two spare minutes are sitting at the end of the talk.
*/}

## what role did you play?

![bg right:40%](./assets/bg-core-insight-portrait.avif)

the pairs on your page → the **parameters** of the model

the hands in the air → the **probability distribution**

my random pick → **sampling**

the sentence I wrote → **generation**

{/* notes:
Three clicks. Hemingway, let it land. Poe, watch it dawn. Seuss last --- the biggest laugh, wait it out.

Nobody in this room was told there were three books. Neither was the model.

Then stay here and say the rest of it --- there is no slide for the crossing, and there shouldn't be, because the three fragments on the wall are the evidence. Each book was counted separately; no pair spans the join. But you already watched it cross: every time I called `said`, the hands that went up were holding Hemingway, Seuss and Poe, and neither you nor the model could tell which was which. This room was the seam.

And the old man. `old` then `man` is a pair in two of these books --- Hemingway's fisherman, dozens of times, and Poe's victim, a dozen more --- and they land in the same place on your sheets. The model has one token of memory; it cannot see which book it's in, because "which book" was never something it recorded. As far as this model is concerned, they are the same old man.

If the sentence you wrote visibly crossed, arrow back to slide 6 and point at the join. Every strange sentence you have ever got out of a chatbot is this, at scale.
*/}
