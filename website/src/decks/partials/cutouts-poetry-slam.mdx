# Poetry slam

![bg](./assets/bg-poetry-slam.avif)

```notes
The open-ended capstone: design a model, swap, build, perform. Keep it moving.
```

---

## The challenge

![bg right:40%](./assets/bg-design-challenge.avif)

design a poetry language model from scratch

write your algorithm (recipe, or procedure) down on paper; another group will build it and
perform the result

your model card plus their performance is the completion task

```notes
They design a poetry model on paper; another group builds and performs it. There is no single right answer.
```

---

## Design your model (20 min)

![bg right:40%](./assets/bg-design-your-model.avif)

**in pairs**

what _isn't_ poetry?

what does _your_ model do, and why?

write up your model on a blank lesson card to give to another group

```notes
Step back and let them design. Nudge "what isn't poetry?" if a group stalls. 20 min, circulate.
```

---

## Pre-slam prep (10 min)

![bg right:40%](./assets/bg-prep-rehearsal.avif)

swap lesson cards with another group

you have **10 minutes** to generate as much text as you can from their model,
then plan a one-minute performance

```notes
Swap cards; 10 min to generate from another group's model and plan a one-minute performance.
```

# The slam

![bg](./assets/bg-stage-performance.avif)

```notes
Each group performs for ~1 min. Keep it snappy, clap everyone. ~15 min for the room.
```

## Discussion

![bg left:40%](./assets/bg-dialogue.avif)

hardest part of **designing** your model?

hardest part of **preparing** the performance?

how does this relate to Claude/ChatGPT/Gemini: similarities and differences?

what does it all mean?

```notes
Let them surface the Claude/ChatGPT parallels themselves. ~5 min.
```

## The _language_ of language models

![bg right:60%](./assets/bg-vocabulary.avif)

temperature

prompt engineering

```notes
Everyday sense first, then ours: temperature is how hot something is; here it's how random the next-word choice is. Engineering is bridges and machines; prompt engineering is wording the input to get the output you want, where "prompt" is the cue you give an actor.
```
