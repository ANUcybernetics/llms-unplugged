## The recipe

![bg right:40%](./assets/bg-algorithm-generation.avif)

```notes
1. **choose** any starting word from your grid
2. **look** at that word's row to find all possible next words and their counts
3. **roll dice** weighted by the counts to pick the next word
4. **write down** the chosen word and make it your new starting word
5. **repeat** from step 2
```

use your grid to generate new text, rolling dice to choose each next word

## Your turn

![bg right:40%](./assets/bg-generation-chain.avif)

write down a starting token, then repeat:

1. **find the row** for the last token you wrote
2. **share the ten faces** of the d10 across that row's cells, by their tallies
3. **roll** to see which band it lands in (skip the roll if there's only one option)
4. **write it down**, and look up its row next

# Shareback

![bg](./assets/bg-div-reception.avif)

```notes
Take a couple of groups' generated sentences; celebrate the weird ones. ~5 min.

If the room has energy for it, close the shareback by turning it outward: does that change how you picture what's happening when you type into ChatGPT? Then wait; the silence is longer than feels comfortable and the first answer usually arrives just after you'd have given up. If nothing comes, narrow it: "did anything about that feel like something you've seen it do?"

Don't answer your own question, and when someone raises something technical, flip it back before you take it: "what draws you to that?" or "has anyone else run into that?" The expertise in the room is more use to them than a lecture from the front.
```

## The _language_ of language models

![bg cover left:60%](./assets/bg-shannon.avif)

- prompt
- completion

```notes
Everyday sense first, then ours: to prompt is to cue an actor who's forgotten a line; here the prompt is the starting text you give the model. A completion is finishing something off; here it's the text the model generates back. A hallucination is seeing something that isn't there, a malfunction, yet the sentence you just generated wasn't in the training text either and came from the same tallies and dice as everything else. The model runs one process throughout; we call the output a hallucination when the answer is wrong or unwanted.
```
