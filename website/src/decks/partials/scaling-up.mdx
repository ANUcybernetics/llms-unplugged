## Attention: learning where to look

![bg right:40%](./assets/bg-attention.avif)

<div class="context-scale">
  <div class="context-row">
    <span class="context-tokens">
      <span class="token attn-weak">the</span>

      <span class="token attn-medium">cabinet</span>
      <span class="context-next">?</span>
    </span>
  </div>
</div>

from `cabinet` alone you'd guess _is_---but it's _are_, because of `keys`, four
words back

**attention** lets every earlier word vote on what comes next, and the model
_learns_ the weights---no grid could ever be that big

{/* notes:
The transformer's central trick, and the answer to the blown-up grid: instead of a row for every possible context, each prediction looks back over the whole context and weighs which words matter. For "are" vs "is" it's the subject, not the nearest noun. Hands-on version: the Induction Heads lesson on the website is a real attention pattern --- "find the last time I saw this word, copy what followed" --- that you can run with pen and paper.
*/}

your grid stores each pattern as **tally marks you can count**; a transformer
spreads the same patterns across **billions of tunable numbers**---knobs nudged
during training, so similar words like `dog` and `cat` come to share them

{/* notes:
Your grid stores patterns as tally marks you can point to. A transformer spreads them across billions of numbers (its parameters), each nudged a tiny amount after every wrong guess in training. That spreading is why it generalises --- "dog" and "cat" end up sharing patterns. The cost: you can no longer point to where any one pattern lives.
*/}

everything you built only **continues** text---**post-training** on example
conversations is what turns a _continuer_ into an _assistant_

{/* notes:
A pretrained model is a text-continuer, not an assistant --- prompt it with a question and a plausible continuation is more questions. The chat behaviour comes from a second, much smaller phase of training on example conversations plus feedback on which answers people preferred. Same predict-the-next-word mechanism underneath; different data, different habits on top.
*/}
