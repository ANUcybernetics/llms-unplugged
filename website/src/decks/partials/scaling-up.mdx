## Why not just a bigger grid?

with a real model's vocabulary of ~50,000 tokens:

| context          | rows in the grid                     |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| 1 word           | 50,000                               |
| 2 words          | 2.5 billion                          |
| 3 words          | 125 trillion                         |
| a whole sentence | more rows than atoms in the universe |

every extra word of context multiplies the grid by the whole vocabulary---you
can't tally your way to a long memory

{/* notes:
The point isn't the numbers, it's the shape: exponential blow-up. Almost every row would also be empty --- most 3-word sequences never occur in any training text. A bigger lookup table is the wrong tool; you need a different mechanism.
*/}

## Attention: learning where to look

  <div class="context-row">

      <span class="context-next">?</span>
    </span>
  </div>
</div>

from `cabinet` alone, your bigram would guess _is_---but the next word is
_are_, because of `keys`, four words back

**attention** lets every earlier word vote on what comes next---and the model
_learns_ how big each word's vote should be

{/* notes:
This is the transformer's central trick, and the answer to the blown-up grid: instead of a row for every possible context, each prediction looks back over the whole context and weighs which words matter. For "are" vs "is" it's the subject, not the nearest noun. If anyone wants a hands-on version: the Induction Heads lesson on the website is a real attention pattern --- "find the last time I saw this word, copy what followed" --- that you can run with pen and paper.
*/}

## Tallies become knobs

![bg right:40%](./assets/bg-paper-mechanism.avif)

your grid stores its patterns as tally marks you can point to

a transformer stores them in **billions of numbers** (its _parameters_), each
nudged a tiny amount after every wrong guess during training

and similar words share patterns: what the model learns about `dog` also helps
it with `cat`

{/* notes:
Training is still counting-ish --- guess the next word, compare with the actual text, adjust --- but the adjustment is spread across billions of dials rather than one tally in one cell. That spreading is why it generalises: "dog" and "cat" end up nearby, so patterns transfer. The cost: you can no longer point to where any one pattern lives.
*/}

## From autocomplete to assistant

![bg right:40%](./assets/bg-dialogue.avif)

everything so far just **continues text**---ask a raw model a question and it
may well continue with more questions

the helpfulness is trained in afterwards (_post-training_): example
conversations, plus human feedback on which answers are better

{/* notes:
A pretrained model is a text-continuer, not an assistant --- prompt it with "what is the capital of France?" and a plausible continuation is a list of more quiz questions. The chat behaviour comes from a second, much smaller phase of training on conversations and preference feedback. Same mechanism underneath; different habits on top.
*/}
