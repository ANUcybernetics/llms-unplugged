## Things that catch people out

![bg right:40%](./assets/bg-ledger-sheet.avif)

`.` is a word: the row for `.` says how sentences **start**

a new word goes in the **next empty box** --- don't skip boxes, the colours go
in order

**one row per word**, and say it out loud when you start one, or two people will
keep counts of the same word

```notes
Now every group builds its own model from a new text on blank sheets. Watch for two snags:
- skipping an empty box breaks the link between box position and counter colour
- on blank sheets two people can easily start a row for "the", splitting its tallies across two sheets

Every text is written so no word has more than four distinct followers; if a group thinks they need a fifth box, they have doubled up or misread a pair.
```

## Your turn

![bg right:40%](./assets/bg-tally-training.avif)

read your new text **two words at a time**, and for each pair:

1. **find the row** for the first word --- no row yet? start one, and say the
   word out loud
2. **find the box** for the second word --- not there yet? write it in the next
   empty box
3. **add a mark** in that box
4. **move along by one**: the second word starts the next pair

<Timer minutes={10} overlay />

```notes
Leave this up while they work. Hand out the blank sheets and training texts, one per group (a different text for each group, for the class story at the end). Nothing is pre-printed on the sheets, so have them agree on how to claim new words before starting: whoever has a spare row takes the new word and calls it out so the group knows who holds it. On the reader's text page, a solid box marks the first time a word appears (start a new row) and a dashed box means its row already exists on someone's sheet. Have the reader call out one pair and wait for "done" before moving on.
```
