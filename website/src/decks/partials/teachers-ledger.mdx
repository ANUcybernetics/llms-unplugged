## Running it in your class

![bg right:40%](./assets/bg-group-activity.avif)

groups of 4--5, each with a **different picture book**

**60 minutes**: the picture books, counters, a cup and a tub per group, and a
whiteboard for training "the" together

**90 minutes**: add blank sheets and a new text per group, then the class story

everything to print is in the pack on the lesson page

```notes
Step out of the student role here, and say so. Then the logistics, briefly, and hand over to their questions --- they will have them.

The snags you saw today are the ones their class will hit: counters left in the cup from the last draw, a word with no row (start again from any word), and in the 90-minute version two people starting a row for the same word. The whiteboard tally in the 60-minute version is a good job for a student. With more than five groups the books repeat; spread the repeats around the room.
```

## “So is ChatGPT just counting?”

![bg right:40%](./assets/bg-teach-model.avif)

the **same loop**: look at the words so far, weigh the next ones, pick one at
random

it looks at the whole conversation, not one word, and learned from trillions of
words, not one book

its patterns are **shared numbers**, not one tally per pair, so it can write
things nobody ever wrote

```notes
The question their students will ask, and the honest answer for a classroom. Yes, at heart: every reply is sampled one word at a time, which is why "regenerate" gives a different answer, just as two groups with the same book wrote different stories. But don't stop at "just": the differences are scale (the context it looks at, the text it learned from) and the shared numbers, which let a sentence about a cat help with one about a dog. Then a second round of training on examples of good answers, which is what turns "continue this text" into answering a question. The paper model is the real mechanism at small scale, not a cartoon of it.
```
