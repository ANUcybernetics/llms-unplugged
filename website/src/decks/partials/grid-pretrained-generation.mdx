## Why do it again?

![bg right:40%](./assets/bg-more-context.avif)

**same recipe, more training text**: yours learnt from a few sentences, this one
learnt from a whole novel

first step on the road to the very big ones

```notes
From where they're sitting, the next twenty minutes can look like a repeat of the exercise they just did. Frame it as the first step up in scale: their grid, then a whole book in the booklet, then Frankenstein at full size in the scaling-up section, then a frontier model. Every step runs the same loop with more data; doing this one by hand lets them see what changes and what stays the same.

Ask them to read their output as they generate: it will sound more like real prose, yet still won't be about anything. The booklet's stretch goal works well if the room needs a hook: try to guess the training text before checking the title on the cover.
```

## The recipe

![bg right:40%](./assets/bg-algorithm-generation-2.avif)

```notes
1. **choose** any bold word in the booklet as your starting word
2. **look up** that word's entry to see possible next words and their thresholds
3. **roll** your d10(s) and scan the thresholds to find the next word
4. **write down** the chosen word and make it your new starting word
5. **repeat** from step 2
```

use the booklet to generate new text, rolling one or more d10s to choose each
next word

# Shareback

![bg](./assets/bg-div-reception-2.avif)

```notes
Compare with the hand-built model: a bigger training text gives more varied, natural-sounding output. Take about 5 groups. ~5 min.

Ask the room: it reads better, but is it *saying* anything? More data bought fluency: the booklet's output sounds like prose because Paterson sounds like prose, yet it comes from the same tally-and-roll mechanism. Fluency isn't evidence of understanding. Take one or two answers and move on to scaling up.
```

## The _language_ of language models

![bg right:60%](./assets/bg-language-of-lms.avif)

- pre-trained model
- open weight model

```notes
Everyday sense first, then ours: the weights are the numbers inside the model (in the booklet, the thresholds derived from the tallies), and *open weight* means anyone can download a copy of those numbers. You can take the booklet home, read every number in it, and generate from it at the kitchen table; no-one can switch it off or change it under you. With most commercial chatbots, your text goes to someone else's servers, an answer comes back, and the weights stay behind closed doors. If anyone asks: open weight is narrower than open source (you get the numbers, not the training text or the training code), and running a frontier open-weight model still takes serious hardware, whereas the booklet only needs a d10.
```
