## Now they're just a pile

<CutoutsOverview tokens={OVERVIEW_TOKENS} mode="scatter" seed={4} />

```notes
The "every pair becomes a cutout" (flow) slide before these also carries the pile
directive, so the shuffle begins there as each cutout glides from its reading-order
slot into the first scattered pile. Five "scatter" frames of the *same* cutouts
follow with different seeds. All carry `{/* _animate: pile */}`, so Reveal.js
auto-animate glides each cutout (matched by its data-id="cut-N") from one
arrangement to the next; each click reshuffles the pile while the heading stays
put. Talk across them: the model is an unordered bag you rummage through, so
word order in the original text stops mattering once the cutouts are separated.
Add or remove `{/* _animate: pile */}` + `<CutoutsOverview mode="scatter" seed={N}/>`
slides (any seed) to lengthen or shorten the shuffle.
```

## Train your model

![bg right:40%](./assets/bg-cut-spread.avif)

with your group you'll need **printed cutouts**, **scissors**, and a **clear
table**

cut out the cutouts for your text and spread them on the table; each cutout
pairs a **next word** with its **previous word**:

<Cutout prev="green" next="eggs" />

the spread _is_ your trained **language model**

```notes
Once the loose-on-table flow makes sense, groups can optionally sort their cutouts into piles by previous word; it makes hunting for matches faster in the next section.
```
