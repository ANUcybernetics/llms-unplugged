## Every text, its own model

![bg right:40%](./assets/bg-many-texts.avif)

each group trained on a different text, so each group's row for `the` is
different

together the class has a **bigger model** than any one group

```notes
Ask each group what came after "the" in their text. Nobody has "kookaburra" except the kookaburra group; nobody has "yabby" except the creek group, or "kitten" except the big blue ball group. The class as a whole knows all of them.
```

## How we'll do it

![bg right:40%](./assets/bg-bucket.avif)

I'll call out the **current word**

**hands up** if your sheets have a row for it --- I'll pick a group

that group **reads out its colours and tallies**, we fill the cup up here and
draw: their word is the **class's next word**

```notes
Two steps per word: the show of hands picks the group, and the cup picks the word. Only the cup moves up the front: the group calls out its row ("three red, one blue") and you count the counters in and draw, which is much quicker than twenty tables filling and tipping out cups one at a time. Whoever's row it is reads out the word beside the colour you draw. Spread the picks around; choosing a group with a rare word lets the class story surprise people. Write each word on the butchers paper as it lands. It becomes the current word, the next one you call.
```

the class story grows by one word, and the new word is the **current word**

```notes
"Two red, two blue, two green, two yellow": eight counters in, and yellow comes out. Ask that group what their yellow word is ("yabby"). Keep going for a few sentences. When a word only one group has (yabby, kookaburra, gull) comes out, the class model says something most groups never saw.
```
