## Your goal

![bg right:40%](./assets/bg-bag-draw.avif)

write down a starting word, then repeat:

1. find the row for the last word you wrote
2. **load the bag**: one counter per mark, in the colour of its strip
3. **draw one** without looking --- its colour names the next word
4. write it down, tip the bag back into the tub, and hand over to whoever has
   the next row

```notes
Walk through the next slides slowly --- the mechanics are simple but the hand-over ("who has the next row?") is where groups stall. Give every group four jobs: a reader (finds the row), a loader, a drawer, and a writer. If you have a real bag and a visualiser, do the draws for real and use the slides as the backup.
```

```notes
Hand out the bags and tubs now. The row for "." is the honest place to start (it says how the book starts sentences), but any word will do.
```

## Activity

![bg right:40%](./assets/bg-activity-generation.avif)

generate as much story as you can --- when you hit a `.` or `?` you like, start
a new sentence from any word

```notes
Circulate. Common stalls: forgetting to tip the bag back into the tub between draws (the counters from the last row are still in there); a row with no marks at all (the word only ever ended the text --- start again from any word); arguing about which strip a colour is (read the name printed in the strip's corner).
```

## Different books, different stories

![bg right:40%](./assets/bg-information-flow-generation.avif)

read out your first sentence --- can the rest of the room guess your book?

two groups with the **same book**: why aren't your stories the same?

could any group's model write a story about a **crocodile**?

```notes
Pick four or five groups, not all of them. Three probes. First: the stories sound like their books, because the model only has the words, and the pairs, that its book had. Second: two groups with the same sheets gave two different stories --- the bag is random, and a model that's asked twice gives two answers (the wrap-up comes back to this). Third: no crocodile anywhere on any sheet, so no model in the room can say it. Then the hinge: so where did these marks come from? Someone counted. Next section: you're the ones counting.
```
