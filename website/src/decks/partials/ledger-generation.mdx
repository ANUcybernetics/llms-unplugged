## Your goal

![bg right:40%](./assets/bg-bag-draw.avif)

write down a starting word, then repeat:

1. find the row for the last word you wrote
2. **load the bag**: one ball per mark, in the colour of its strip
3. **draw one** without looking --- its colour names the next word
4. write it down, tip the bag out, and hand over to whoever has the next row

```notes
Walk through the next slides slowly --- the mechanics are simple but the hand-over ("who has the next row?") is where groups stall. Assign one person to be the writer.
```

## Activity

![bg right:40%](./assets/bg-activity-generation.avif)

generate as much story as you can --- when you hit a `.` or `?` you like, start a new sentence from any word

```notes
Circulate. Common stalls: forgetting to tip the bag out between draws (the balls from the last row are still in there); a row with no marks at all (the word only ever ended the text --- start again from any word); arguing about which strip a colour is (read the name printed in the strip's corner).
```

## Same model, different stories

![bg right:40%](./assets/bg-information-flow-generation.avif)

every group started from `I` --- read out your first sentence

why aren't they the same?

could any group's model write a story about a **crocodile**?

```notes
Two probes. First: the same sheets in five groups gave five different stories --- the bag is random, and a model that's asked twice gives two answers (the wrap-up comes back to this). Second: no crocodile anywhere on any sheet, so the model cannot say it. It only has the words, and the pairs, that its text had. Then the hinge: so where did these marks come from? Someone counted. Next section: you're the ones counting.
```
