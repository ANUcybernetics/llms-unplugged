<div class="ledger-step">
  <LedgerRow entry={ROW_COMES} palette={PALETTE} highlight={0} />

no cup needed: after `comes` this book only ever said `.`

```notes
A one-word row needs no draw at all. Groups will hit a few of these and they speed things up. Eight draws in, and we have a sentence this book never said: it says "See it go up." and it says "Down it comes.", and the model has glued the two together, because it only ever knows what comes next, one word at a time. Stop at a full stop or a question mark you like, or keep going.
```

## Your turn

![bg right:40%](./assets/bg-bag-draw.avif)

write down a starting word, then repeat:

1. **find the row** for the last word you wrote
2. **fill the cup**: one counter for every tally mark, each in its word's colour
3. **draw one** without looking --- its colour tells you the next word
4. **write it down**, tip the cup back into the tub, and pass the job to
   whoever has that word's row

```notes
This is the whole loop on one slide --- leave it up while they work. Four jobs per group: a reader who finds the row, a filler, a drawer, and a writer.
```

Hand out the cups and tubs now. The row for "." is the honest place to start (it says how the book starts sentences), but any word will do.

## Activity

![bg right:40%](./assets/bg-activity-generation.avif)

generate as much story as you can --- when you hit a `.` or `?` you like, start
a new sentence from any word

```notes
Circulate. Common stalls: forgetting to tip the cup back into the tub between draws (the counters from the last row are still in there); a row with no marks at all (the word only ever ended the text --- start again from any word); arguing about which box a colour is (read the name printed in the box's corner).
```

## No two stories the same

![bg right:40%](./assets/bg-information-flow-generation.avif)

read out your first sentence --- can the rest of the room guess your book?

two groups with the **same book**: why aren't your stories the same?

could any group's model write a story about a **crocodile**?

```notes
Pick four or five groups, not all of them. Three probes. First: the stories sound like their books, because the model only has the words, and the pairs, that its book had. Second: two groups with the same sheets gave two different stories --- the cup is random, and a model that's asked twice gives two answers (the wrap-up comes back to this). Third: no crocodile anywhere on any sheet, so no model in the room can say it. Then the hinge: so where did these marks come from? Someone counted. Next section: you're the ones counting.
```
