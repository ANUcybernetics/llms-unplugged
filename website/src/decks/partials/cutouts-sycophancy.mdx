## What is sycophancy?

![bg right:40%](./assets/bg-yes-figure.avif)

a model that **always agrees with you**:

> "you're absolutely right"
>
> "that's a great insight"
>
> "what a thoughtful question"

real LLMs are notoriously prone to it---partly from RLHF (human raters reward
agreeable answers), partly from training data (the internet is full of
flattery)

{/* notes:
Real LLMs do this too --- the slide says why. Don't over-explain; the activity makes the point.
*/}

## Your goal

![bg right:40%](./assets/bg-confluence.avif)

add a stack of **sycophancy cutouts** to your existing spread, regenerate from
the same starting word, and watch the output drift toward agreement

think of it as pooling two models---your training text + a curated sycophancy
model---into one

## Worked example

the sycophancy cutouts encode patterns like:

<CutoutRow>
  <Cutout prev="you're" next="absolutely" />
  <Cutout prev="absolutely" next="right" />
  <Cutout prev="right" next="." />
</CutoutRow>

common words (<Token next>you</Token>, <Token next>that</Token>,
<Token next>i</Token>, <Token next>.</Token>) bridge between the two
models---once generation crosses into the sycophancy stack, the output drifts:

> before: _"i am sam . sam i am ."_
>
> after: _"i am absolutely right . that 's a great insight ."_

same mechanism, just more cutouts pulling generation toward flattery

{/* notes:
Common words (you, that, i, .) bridge into the sycophancy stack; once the chain crosses over, the output drifts toward flattery.
*/}

## The _language_ of language models

![bg right:60%](./assets/bg-vocabulary.avif)

RLHF

alignment

training-data bias

helpful / honest / harmless

{/* notes:
Gloss each: RLHF = people rate answers, the model learns to prefer the winners; alignment = getting it to do what we actually want; training-data bias = it copies whatever's in the data; helpful/honest/harmless = the goal we tune toward.
