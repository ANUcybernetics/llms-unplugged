# A language model on paper

![bg](./assets/bg-ledger-title.avif)

```notes
Hand out the sheets now, one per person. Keep the bags of counters back until the activity slide --- nobody is told what the colours mean yet, and a bag on the table is a bag on the floor.
```

{/* _animate: sheet */}

## This is a language model

<LedgerSheet entries={SHEET_THREE} palette={PALETTE} />

one sheet of it: a word down the left, and beside it every word that came next

```notes
Take your time here. Left column: words from the book. Along each row: the words that followed, and a mark for every time the book did it. That's the whole thing --- no rules, no grammar, just a count of what came next. Let them look at it before you touch a single row.
```

---

{/* _animate: sheet */}

## One row at a time

<LedgerSheet entries={SHEET_THREE} palette={PALETTE} focus={3} />

after the word `I`, this book said `am` eight times, `do` five times, and
`would` twice

```notes
Read the row aloud as a sentence. The Green Eggs groups can find this exact row; every other group finds the row for a word they like in their own book and reads it the same way. Two or three groups read theirs out.
```

---

## Your group holds the whole model

<LedgerSheets ranges={SHEET_RANGES} palette={PALETTE} />

every sheet holds a run of words in alphabetical order --- the header says which

to find a word, read the headers: **who has `like`?**

```notes
These are the Green Eggs headers; every book deals differently, so each group practises on its own. Two lookups each: "who has like?" (Green Eggs: sheet 3), "who has Sam?" (sheet 4). The header is the whole trick --- nobody has to search, they just read ranges.
```

---

## The colours

<LedgerRow entry={ROW_I} firstRow={3} palette={PALETTE} highlight={1} />

each strip has a colour, and the counters in your bag come in the same colours

a purple counter means **the word beside the purple strip**

```notes
Now the colours. Hold up a purple counter: on this row, purple means "do". On a different row purple means something else --- the colour names the column, not the word. That's why one bag of counters works for every row, and for every book in the room.
```
