Hand out the sheets now, one per person: every group gets a different picture book. Keep the bags of balls back until the activity slide --- nobody is told what the colours mean yet, and a bag on the table is a bag on the floor.

---

## This is a language model

<LedgerRow entry={ROW_I} firstRow={3} palette={PALETTE} />

one row of it, anyway: after the word `I`, this book said `am` eight times, `do`
five times, and `would` twice

```notes
Read the row aloud as a sentence before saying anything about colours. The Green Eggs groups can find this exact row (it's on the "green → like" sheet); every other group finds the row for a word they like in their own book and reads it the same way. The marks are counts: how many times the book went from this word to that one. That's the entire model --- a table of "what usually comes next".
```

---

## Your group holds the whole model

<LedgerSheets ranges={SHEET_RANGES} palette={PALETTE} />

every sheet holds a run of words in alphabetical order --- the header says which

to find a word, read the headers: **who has `like`?**

```notes
These are the Green Eggs headers; every book deals differently, so each group practises on its own. Two lookups each: "who has like?" (Green Eggs: sheet 3), "who has Sam?" (sheet 4). The header is the whole trick --- nobody has to search, they just read ranges.
```

---

## The colours

<LedgerRow entry={ROW_I} firstRow={3} palette={PALETTE} highlight={1} />

each strip has a colour, and the balls in your bag come in the same colours

a purple ball means **the word beside the purple strip**

```notes
Now the colours. Hold up a purple ball: on this row, purple means "do". On a different row purple means something else --- the colour names the column, not the word. That's why one bag of balls works for every row, and for every book in the room.
```
