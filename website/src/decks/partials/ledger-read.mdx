---

## This is a language model

<LedgerRow entry={ROW_I} firstRow={2} palette={PALETTE} />

one row of it, anyway: after the word `I`, this text said `do` nine times, `am` eight times, and `would` twice

```notes
Read the row aloud as a sentence before saying anything about colours. Ask them to find this row on their sheets (it's on the "here → like" sheet) and read a different row of their own the same way. The marks are counts: how many times the text went from this word to that one. That's the entire model --- a table of "what usually comes next".
```

---

## Your group holds the whole model

<LedgerSheets ranges={SHEET_RANGES} palette={PALETTE} />

every sheet holds a run of words in alphabetical order --- the header says which

to find a word, read the headers: **who has `like`?**

```notes
Practise the lookup twice: "who has like?" (sheet 3), "who has Sam?" (sheet 4). The header is the whole trick --- nobody has to search, they just read ranges.
```

---

## The colours

<LedgerRow entry={ROW_I} firstRow={2} palette={PALETTE} highlight={1} />

each strip has a colour, and the balls in your bag come in the same colours

a blue ball means **the word beside the blue strip**

```notes
Now the colours. Hold up a blue ball: on this row, blue means "do". On a different row blue means something else --- the colour names the column, not the word. That's why one bag of balls works for every row.
```
