# Generation

![bg](./assets/bg-ledger-title.avif)

```notes
Groups of 4-5, if the room isn't in them already --- they stay in those groups all session. Hand out the sheets now, one per person. Keep the counters back until the activity slide --- nobody is told what the colours mean yet, and a cup on the table is a cup on the floor.
```

---

## This is a language model

<LedgerSheet entries={SHEET_FIVE} palette={PALETTE} />

```notes
Take your time here. This is one sheet of it. Left column: words from the book. Along each row: the words that followed, and a mark for every time the book did it. That's the whole thing --- no rules, no grammar, just a count of what came next. Let them look at it before you touch a single row.
```

---

## One row at a time

<LedgerSheet entries={SHEET_FIVE} palette={PALETTE} focus="the" />

```notes
Read the row aloud as a sentence: after the word "the", this book said "magpie" six times, and "fence", "postie" and "dog" four times each. The magpie groups can find this exact row; every other group finds the row for a word they like in their own book and reads it the same way. Two or three groups read theirs out.

The colours go here too: every word sits in a coloured box, and the counters come in those four colours, so a red counter means the word in the red box. Red means "fence" on this row but "the" on the row for "swoops" --- the colour names the place in the row, not the word, which is why one set of counters works for every row and every book.
```

---

## Your group holds the whole model

<LedgerSheets ranges={SHEET_RANGES} palette={PALETTE} />

```notes
Every sheet holds a run of words in alphabetical order, and the header says which. To find a word, read the headers. These are the magpie headers; every book deals differently, so each group practises on its own. Two lookups each: "who has it?" (the magpie: sheet 3), "who has fence?" (sheet 2). The header is the whole trick --- nobody has to search, they just read ranges.
```
