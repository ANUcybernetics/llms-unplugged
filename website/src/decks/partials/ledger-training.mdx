```notes
Five pairs in: the row for "I" already has two marks beside "am", and the second word of each pair became the first word of the next. Keep going until the text runs out and the marks are the model. Then, for the Green Eggs groups: check the "I am" count on your printed sheet --- eight marks --- against the text. Those eight are the eight times the book says "I am" in its first 100 words. ## Train your model
```

![bg right:40%](./assets/bg-five-texts.avif)

each group gets a **new text** and a set of **blank sheets** --- the words down
the side are printed, the marks are yours

one person reads the text **two words at a time**; whoever has the first word's
row adds a mark beside the second

then move along by one word

```notes
Hand out the school-day texts, one per group (a different one each --- that matters for the finale) and the matching sheet sets with prefixes printed. The reader holds the text page: every word is numbered, so "we're at 23" gets a lost group back on track. Tip: the reader says the pair, then waits for the "done" before moving on.
```

---

## Things that catch people out

![bg right:40%](./assets/bg-ledger-sheet.avif)

`.` is a word: the row for `.` says how sentences **start**

a new follower goes in the **next empty cell** --- don't skip cells, the colours
are by column

a **fifth** follower goes on the row below, where your word is printed again in
grey

if the word isn't on your sheet, **read the headers**

```notes
The empty-cell rule is the one to watch: a group that leaves a gap has broken the colour-to-column link and the bag will name the wrong word later. Only the volcano text has a five-follower word ("the"), so only its groups meet the second-row rule; their sheet already has the grey row waiting.
```
