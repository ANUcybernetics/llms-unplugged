## The same pair comes round again

<LedgerTraining tokens={DJ_OPENING} step={8} palette={PALETTE} />

```notes
Skip ahead four pairs, to the second time this text says "see it": the mark goes beside "it" in the row for "see", which already has one. That's how a count builds --- the common pairs collect marks, the rare ones don't. Then, for the Dick and Jane groups: check the "see it" count on your printed sheet --- five marks --- against the whole text. Every other group's sheets were made the same way from their book.
```

## Train your model

![bg right:40%](./assets/bg-five-texts.avif)

each group gets a **new text** and a set of **blank sheets** --- the words down
the side are printed, the marks are yours

one person reads the text **two words at a time**; whoever has the first word's
row adds a mark beside the second

then move along by one word

```notes
Hand out the school-day texts, one per group (a different one each --- that matters for the finale) and the matching sheet sets with prefixes printed. The reader holds the text page: every word is numbered, so "we're at 23" gets a lost group back on track. Tip: the reader says the pair, then waits for the "done" before moving on.
```

---

## Things that catch people out

![bg right:40%](./assets/bg-ledger-sheet.avif)

`.` is a word: the row for `.` says how sentences **start**

a new word goes in the **next empty box** --- don't skip boxes, the colours go
in order

if the word isn't on your sheet, **read the headers**

```notes
The empty-box rule is the one to watch: a group that leaves a gap has broken the colour-to-word link and the cup will name the wrong word later. Every text is chosen so no word gets more than four words after it, so four boxes are always enough; if a group thinks they need a fifth, they have missed one.
```
