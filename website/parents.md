# For parents

![Hero image: For parents](/assets/images/hero-parents.jpg)

Your kids are using ChatGPT. Maybe for homework help, maybe for creative
writing, maybe just to mess around. You've probably used it yourself. But do you
actually understand what's happening when you type a prompt and get a response?

_LLMs Unplugged_ gives you and your family a way to build that understanding
together through hands-on activities. No mystification, no fear-mongering, just
practical knowledge about how these tools actually work.

## Family activity: understanding ChatGPT together

- **time**: 60--90mins
- **for ages**: 12+
- **what you need**: pen, paper, a six-sided die (or spinner), a children's book

Here's a family-friendly version of the core workshop. Start with a brief
introduction, then work through training your own model, using it to generate
text, and finally trying a larger pre-trained model. The flow moves from "how do
patterns get into a model?" to "what comes out?" to "what happens at scale?"

<LessonCards :lessons="['intro', 'basic-training', 'basic-generation', 'pretrained-generation']" />

### Suggested timing

- **00:00 start the conversation** --- who in the family has used ChatGPT or
  similar tools? what for? how do you think it works? where do the words come
  from? today we're going to build our own version---small and simple, but using
  the exact same approach as ChatGPT.

- **00:10 [Basic training](/lessons/basic-training)** --- work together to count
  word patterns in a short text (the lesson provides a simple example, or choose
  a favourite children's book). This is the "training" step---where AI systems
  learn patterns from text.

- **00:25 [Basic generation](/lessons/basic-generation)** --- use your trained
  model and a die to generate new sentences. Take turns rolling and choosing
  words---make it playful. Read out your generated text and laugh at the weird
  results together.

- **00:45 [Pre-trained model generation](/lessons/pretrained-generation)** ---
  try using a bigger, pre-trained model (provided in the lesson). Notice how
  more training data leads to more coherent (and sometimes eerily realistic)
  output.

- **00:55 close the loop** --- what surprised you about how this works? does
  this change how you think about ChatGPT? what questions do you still have?

## Why this matters for families

Understanding how LLMs work helps you have better conversations with your kids
about:

- **homework and learning**: when is it okay to use ChatGPT? what are the
  limitations? how do you check its work?

- **privacy and data**: these models learn from text---what does that mean for
  what you share with them?

- **critical thinking**: if you understand the pattern-matching underneath, you
  can better evaluate when an AI-generated answer might be plausible-sounding
  nonsense

- **career and future**: AI is reshaping work and education---helping your kids
  understand these tools (rather than fear or worship them) is genuinely useful

## Learning alongside your kids

You don't need to be an expert to facilitate these activities. The lessons are
designed to be self-explanatory, and the
[instructor notes](/lessons/basic-training#instructor-notes) (yes, even for
parents!) help you connect the hands-on work to modern AI systems.

Some of the most valuable moments come from exploring together---when you're
both surprised by how simple the core mechanism is, or when you both notice
something strange about the generated text. These "aha" moments build shared
understanding that makes future conversations about AI much more productive.

## Notes

- the activity above is streamlined for home use---if you have more time or
  particularly engaged kids, you can add the
  [Weighted randomness](/lessons/weighted-randomness) lesson at the start (add
  20mins) or the [Sampling](/lessons/sampling) lesson at the end (add 15mins)

- younger kids (under 12) can participate with guidance, especially in the
  generation step---they often love the randomness and silliness of rolling dice
  to make sentences

- if you're doing this with multiple kids, have them each generate their own
  sentences and share them---the variety of outputs from the same model is part
  of the learning

- once you've done the basic activity, the generated text becomes a great
  conversation starter about AI capabilities and limitations over dinner
